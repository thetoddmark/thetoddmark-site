The Wishlist

Tools I'm actively hunting for. Some are common; some I've been watching eBay for months. If you spot one at a reasonable price, you know where to find me.

Some links are Amazon affiliate links (thetoddmark-20) — I earn a small commission at no extra cost to you.

A note for brands & vendors

If you’re a brand or tool supplier and you’d like to send something from this list — or anything else that might make a good restoration subject — please get in touch. I document everything in detail and give honest write-ups to the tools I work with.

A honest note

If you're a maker, seller, or just someone with one of these gathering dust — and you'd like to see it properly restored and documented — I'd be genuinely grateful to give it a good home and a write-up it deserves. I'm not a big creator. I don't have huge numbers. But I care about this work, and every tool on this list would get my full attention. See the Partner page for how this works, or reach out directly via the contact page.

Bandsaw
Inca Bandsaw
Swiss-made precision bandsaws — legendary for their build quality and smooth cuts. Hunting for either an Expert 500 (US Model 710) or a Euro 260 (US Models 310/320/330/340). These rarely show up in good condition and disappear fast when they do. More at incamachines.com.
Actively hunting
Hand Plane
Stanley Bailey No. 1
The smallest Stanley Bailey ever made — only 5½" long and weighing under a pound. More of a collector's piece than a user, but an antique example in honest condition is the goal. One of the hardest Stanley planes to find at a reasonable price.
Casually watching
Measurement
Wagner Orion 950 Moisture Meter
A pinless moisture meter takes the guesswork out of knowing whether wood is ready to work or finish. No holes, no damage to the surface — just a reliable reading. This one's accurate enough to actually trust.
On the list
Workshop Equipment
Trend WRT Workshop Router Table
A proper router table opens up joinery and edge work that's hard to do freehand. The Trend is precision-engineered and designed to grow with a shop over time. Pairs well with the T8 below.
On the list
Measurement
Franklin ProSensor M210 Stud Finder
Thirteen sensors instead of one means it shows you the whole stud, not just guesses at the edge. More accurate than anything I've used and dead simple to operate — no calibration, just hold and scan.
On the list
Power Tool
Metabo HPT SB8V2 Belt Sander
Variable speed and a 3×21" belt is a serious workhorse for material removal and leveling. The kind of tool that saves hours of hand sanding on larger pieces and makes flattening glue-ups actually manageable.
On the list
Clamps
Fulton Panel Max Clamping System
Panel glue-ups are awkward without the right setup. This four-way system applies pressure from all directions and keeps boards flat — a real problem solver when you don't have a dedicated bench or assembly table.
On the list
Power Tool
Milwaukee M18 FUEL Compact Router
Cordless routing sounds like a compromise until you look at Milwaukee's track record. The M18 FUEL line punches above its weight, and not being tethered to an outlet is a genuine quality-of-life upgrade for working in tight spaces.
On the list
Power Tool
Trend T8 Dual-Mode Plunge Router
Dual-mode means it works handheld or in a router table without swapping bases — that flexibility matters when you're building a shop incrementally and don't want to buy two tools to do one job.
On the list

Festool ETS EC 150/5 Random Orbital Sander
Festool is the tool you buy when you're done buying tools. Expensive, over-engineered, and — by all accounts — worth every penny. On the list for someday. A man can dream.
